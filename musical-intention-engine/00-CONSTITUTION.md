# MIE · Constitution

## Core thesis

**Build the shortest possible interface between musical intention and manipulable sound.**

We are investigating what a musical instrument becomes when computation can understand musical intention.

## We are not building

A smaller DAW, social feed, marketplace, publishing network, generic chatbot, or opaque AI song generator.

## Human authorship

The system may interpret, materialize, transform, render, or suggest. The human owns the musical intention and must retain the ability to preserve, compare, revise, and recover it.

Generation without manipulability is not sufficient evidence of authorship-preserving design.

## Mobile first

The phone is not a reduced desktop. Thumb reach, latency, audio feedback, interruption recovery, large targets, and minimal cognitive translation are first-class constraints.

## Musical intention

A human-originated musical impulse that may arrive as sound, gesture, speech, rhythm, melody, texture, or another observable input.

## Musical object

A manipulable representation of musical material that preserves its source, interpretation, provenance, and transformation ancestry.

This definition is provisional and must evolve from experiments.

## Non-negotiables

- preserve source material;
- preserve provenance;
- expose uncertainty;
- keep interpretation replaceable;
- measure interaction rather than praise it;
- prefer reversible changes;
- do not fake verification;
- human authority remains final.

## Product falsifiers

The hypothesis must be reconsidered if evidence shows that users are faster with conventional workflows, musical intention cannot be represented reliably enough to manipulate, transformation destroys identity, or mobile interaction adds more friction than it removes.

## North-star metric

**TTMI: Time To Musical Idea**, from having the idea to hearing and manipulating a representation of it.
