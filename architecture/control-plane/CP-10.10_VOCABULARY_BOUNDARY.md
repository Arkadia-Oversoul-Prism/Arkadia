# CP-10.10 — VOCABULARY BOUNDARY / MECHANISM COMMITMENT

**Status:** ARGUMENT COMPLETE / VOCABULARY BOUNDARY REACHED / NO MECHANISM SELECTED / NO IMPLEMENTATION / NO ENFORCEMENT / REPOSITORY CODE UNTOUCHED

**Parent chain:** `CP-10 → CP-10.1 → CP-10.2 → CP-10.3 → CP-10.4 → CP-10.5 → CP-10.6 → CP-10.7 → CP-10.8 → CP-10.9 → CP-10.10`

## 1. Purpose

CP-10.10 determines what kind of question remains after CP-10.9 failed to instantiate evidence of causal origination.

The question is no longer:

> What semantic properties must evidence of human-origin authority possess?

CP-10.4 through CP-10.6 have already made those properties explicit.

The remaining question is:

> What observable fact establishes that a defined subject caused a specific authority event to originate, without merely encoding or asserting that causal relationship?

CP-10.10 evaluates two possible interpretations of that frontier:

1. **Conjunction-by-necessity:** causal origination cannot be established by any single primitive, so several independently insufficient primitives must jointly establish it.
2. **Vocabulary-boundary:** positive causal origination cannot be completed by vocabulary alone; specifying what observable phenomenon would establish it necessarily commits the architecture to a mechanism class.

## 2. Constitutional question

**Can the vocabulary layer itself instantiate positive causal origination, or has the arc reached the point where any positive answer must name a mechanism-level observable phenomenon?**

The answer must not be obtained by weakening the existing distinctions, redefining provenance, promoting an assertion into evidence, or selecting a mechanism by implication.

## 3. Starting law

The arc now holds three permanent distinctions:

```text
ATTESTATION ≠ ORIGINATION
PRECEDENCE ≠ CAUSATION
EVENT-IDENTITY DEPENDENCE ≠ EVIDENCE OF CAUSAL ORIGINATION
```

It also holds the epistemic separation:

```text
CAUSAL CLAIM
     ↓
CAUSAL FACT
     ↓
EVIDENCE OF CAUSAL FACT
```

CP-10.9 demonstrated that the first can be written, the second may occur, but a written causal claim does not by itself instantiate the third.

## 4. Direction A — Conjunction-by-necessity

### 4.1 Claim

A conjunction-by-necessity argument would establish that no single evidence primitive can carry the complete D3/D4 burden and that multiple independently insufficient primitives must therefore be composed.

### 4.2 What the argument can establish

The arena has already shown that individual classes of evidence can fail to establish the target relation:

- authentication can establish subject association without origination;
- interaction can establish participation without origination;
- attestation can establish deliberate action with respect to an event without establishing that the subject originated the event;
- temporal ordering can establish precedence without causation;
- event identity can encode an alleged origin relation without independently proving it;
- structural integrity can establish artifact continuity without human origin;
- execution can establish downstream occurrence without historical origination.

These results establish that several obvious primitives are insufficient individually.

### 4.3 What the argument cannot establish

The failures above do **not** prove that every possible single primitive is insufficient.

To establish necessity formally, the architecture would need to quantify over the relevant mechanism space and demonstrate that no single primitive can provide the required discriminating observable relation.

That is not a vocabulary-only operation. The candidate space is defined by possible observables and causal structures, not merely by names for evidence classes.

Therefore:

```text
KNOWN INDIVIDUAL FAILURES
        ≠
PROOF THAT ALL SINGLE PRIMITIVES MUST FAIL
```

A conjunction may be a plausible future architecture. It is not a theorem earned by CP-10.9.

### 4.4 Verdict on Direction A

**NOT ESTABLISHED.**

Conjunction-by-necessity remains a candidate design hypothesis, not a demonstrated architectural necessity.

## 5. Direction B — Vocabulary-boundary argument

### 5.1 Claim

The vocabulary layer can specify the semantic target and the conditions a successful mechanism must satisfy, but it cannot instantiate a positive causal observable without specifying what observable phenomenon constitutes the evidence.

### 5.2 Why this follows from the arc

CP-10.4 defined the origin evidence vector:

```text
OE = [SUBJECT, EVENT, ORIGIN_RELATION, DELIBERATE_ORIGIN, TIME_CONTEXT]
```

CP-10.6 sharpened D3 and D4 by requiring discrimination between origination and association, and between deliberate origination and interaction, automation, replay, delegation, inheritance, derivation, and downstream execution.

CP-10.7 then required a mechanism candidate to provide a positive instantiation of those relations.

CP-10.8 failed because deliberate attestation did not establish origination.

CP-10.9 failed because event-existence dependence was asserted or defined, but no non-circular observable primitive established the causal dependence.

At this point, adding more vocabulary of the same type cannot solve the problem. It can only restate the target relation, add exclusions, or rename the missing observable.

### 5.3 The boundary test

For any proposed technology-neutral refinement, ask:

> Does this refinement tell the evaluator what observable fact establishes `SUBJECT → ORIGINATED → EVENT`, or does it merely restate properties that evidence would need to have?

If it merely restates the properties, it remains vocabulary.

If it answers what observable phenomenon establishes the relation, it has crossed into mechanism commitment.

This is not a defect. It is the boundary being measured.

## 6. Non-circularity test

The following forms remain insufficient:

```text
EVENT_CREATED_BY = S
```

unless the architecture can independently establish why that statement is true.

Likewise:

```text
EVENT_ID = f(S, A)
```

does not establish that S caused E merely because E's identity was constructed from S and A.

And:

```text
A happened before E
```

does not establish causal dependence.

And:

```text
E exists because A caused E
```

is a causal claim, not independent evidence of the causal fact.

The vocabulary layer can reject these forms. It cannot manufacture the missing observable.

## 7. Why the conjunction argument cannot close the boundary

A conjunction is itself a mechanism-level composition question.

Suppose a future candidate proposes:

```text
P1 + P2 + P3 + P4 → ORIGIN-PROVEN
```

The arena must still ask:

- What does P1 observe?
- What does P2 observe?
- What does P3 observe?
- What does P4 observe?
- Which false positive does each exclude?
- What relation emerges from their conjunction that does not exist in the individual primitives?
- Why does the conjunction discriminate World A from Worlds B/C?
- What prevents the conjunction from merely accumulating mutually reinforcing assertions?

Those questions cannot be answered by saying "the primitives are independent". Independence itself requires a defined mechanism or observable basis.

Therefore the conjunction argument does not remain purely vocabulary-level.

## 8. The boundary theorem

Within the scope of this arc, the following is now established:

> **A technology-neutral vocabulary can define causal origination as the target relation, define the dimensions and exclusions required for evidence of that relation, and falsify semantic substitutes for it. It cannot positively instantiate the observable phenomenon that establishes the causal relation without specifying what class of observable mechanism produces that phenomenon.**

This is the vocabulary boundary.

The statement does **not** claim that causal origination is impossible to establish.

It claims only that establishing it requires the architecture to move from semantic specification into mechanism-prescriptive evaluation.

## 9. What CP-10.10 closes

CP-10.10 closes the following question at the vocabulary layer:

**Answer: No.**

The semantic target is sufficiently specified for mechanism evaluation. Further progress requires a mechanism candidate that names an observable phenomenon and can be tested against the existing arena.
