## 1. Candidate question

Can human causal origination be discriminated by a conjunction of two or more evidence classes that are each insufficient alone, but jointly necessary for the target claim?

The candidate is intentionally mechanism-neutral. It does not name biometrics, hardware, cryptography, trusted devices, witnesses, enclaves, or any other implementation mechanism.

The target claim remains:

`HUMAN_ORIGIN(S,P)`

where `S` is the subject and `P` is the relevant precondition or authority-bearing event.

The candidate asks whether there can exist independently inspectable evidence classes `A` and `B` such that:

`A(S,P) ∧ B(S,P) → evidence sufficient for HUMAN_ORIGIN(S,P)`

while:

`A alone` is insufficient,
`B alone` is insufficient,
and the conjunction is not merely two correlated assertions of the same underlying fact.

This is a test of evidentiary structure, not a declaration that such a conjunction exists.

---

## 2. Governing distinctions

The candidate must preserve all prior distinctions:

- `ATTESTATION ≠ ORIGINATION`
- `PRECEDENCE ≠ CAUSATION`
- `EVENT-IDENTITY DEPENDENCE ≠ EVIDENCE OF CAUSAL ORIGINATION`
- `PROTOCOL CAUSATION ≠ HUMAN CAUSAL ORIGINATION`
- `EVIDENCE EXISTS ≠ EVIDENCE DISCRIMINATES ≠ EVIDENCE IS SUFFICIENT`
- `PROVENANCE ≠ AUTHORIZATION ≠ EXECUTION`
- `HUMAN ORIGINATION ≠ AUTHORITY`
- `INDEPENDENTLY INSPECTABLE ≠ INDEPENDENTLY HUMAN-ORIGINATED`

The candidate therefore cannot pass merely because two records agree, because two systems attest the same subject, because an event follows an interaction, or because the protocol requires both records.

---

## 3. Positive candidate structure

A valid conjunction candidate would require five properties.

### C1 — Complementarity

`A` and `B` must contribute distinguishable information. `B` cannot simply restate, re-encode, or deterministically derive `A`.

### C2 — Individual insufficiency

Neither `A` nor `B`, considered alone, may establish `HUMAN_ORIGIN(S,P)`.

### C3 — Joint sufficiency

The conjunction must discriminate the human-origin world from the relevant system-generated counterfactual world.

### C4 — Independence of evidentiary generation

The system must not be able to generate both `A` and `B` through one ordinary autonomous path while preserving the same observations.

### C5 — Counterfactual discrimination

There must be an observable difference between:

**World H:** the human performs the relevant originating act, producing `A` and `B`, after which `P` exists and the event proceeds.

**World S:** the subject is merely associated with the session or event while the system autonomously produces `A` and `B`, after which `P` exists and the event proceeds.

If World H and World S can produce observationally equivalent evidence, the conjunction does not establish human origination.

---

## 4. Counterfactual test

The decisive test is not whether `A ∧ B` is hard to fabricate. The test is whether the evidence contains an observable relation that is present because the human originated `P`, and absent when the system originated `P` without that human act.

### World H

`S performs originating act → A + B → P → E`

### World S

`S is associated with context → system performs originating act → A + B → P → E`

### Required result

There must exist some observable predicate `Δ` such that:

`Δ(World H) = true`

and

`Δ(World S) = false`

for the relevant class of system-generated counterfactuals.

If:

`Obs(World H) = Obs(World S)`

then:

`E2 = FAIL`

and the candidate is not eligible.

---

## 5. Anti-double-counting rule

Two evidence classes do not become independent merely because they have different names, storage locations, timestamps, providers, or schemas.

The candidate must reject the following forms of false conjunction:

1. **Duplicate assertion:** two systems independently state `origin=S`.
2. **Mirrored attestation:** B is derived from A through another signer or service.
3. **Protocol decomposition:** one causal relation is split into two fields and then counted twice.
4. **Session coupling:** both records inherit the same authenticated session and therefore add no independent origin information.
5. **Shared automation:** one autonomous process emits both A and B.
6. **Derived evidence:** B is computed from A or from the same underlying event.
7. **Post-hoc corroboration:** B is created after P or E and merely confirms an already-existing event.
8. **Delegated production:** an authorized agent creates both evidence classes on behalf of S.
9. **Replay/reproduction:** previously valid A and B are reproduced in a new context.
10. **Consensus laundering:** multiple agreeing observers are treated as proof of origination when all observe only the same system-generated event.

Therefore:

`MULTIPLE RECORDS ≠ MULTIPLE CAUSES ≠ INDEPENDENT EVIDENCE`.

## 6. Evidence-state matrix

### E1 — Evidence presence

**PASS** if the candidate specifies at least two non-identical evidence classes.

### E2 — Evidence discrimination

**PASS** only if the conjunction distinguishes World H from World S under the decisive counterfactual.

### E3 — Evidence sufficiency

**PASS** only if the conjunction establishes human causal origination rather than merely increasing confidence in association, identity, authorization, or protocol validity.

### Independence

A further gate applies:

`INDEPENDENT GENERATION ≠ INDEPENDENT INSPECTION`

The candidate must show why the evidentiary components cannot both be produced by the same autonomous source while preserving all protocol-visible observations.

---

## 7. D3 / D4 burden

### D3 — Positive human-origin discrimination

The candidate must show a positive observable distinction attributable to human origination.

The following do not satisfy D3 by themselves:

- authenticated subject;
- deliberate interaction;
- successful challenge response;
- session ownership;
- valid precondition;
- attested device or environment;
- timestamp ordering;
- event causation within the protocol;
- multiple agreeing records.

### D4 — System-generated false-positive exclusion

The candidate must remain discriminating against:

- autonomous generation;
- authentication-triggered generation;
- delegated generation;
- background/admin generation;
- replay or reproduction;
- inherited/pre-existing state;
- derived generation;
- synthetic generation preserving protocol-visible fields;
- automation capable of producing both evidence classes.

A conjunction that excludes only some of these classes is incomplete unless the unresolved classes are explicitly outside the candidate's claimed target domain.

## 10. Anti-laundering gates

The following laundering paths remain forbidden:

- `approved=true → human-origin=true`;
- PassSpec wrapper → human-origin=true;
- duplicated assertions → independent origin evidence;
- downstream authorization → upstream provenance;
- accumulation of weak associations → causal proof;
- successful execution → proof of origination;
- protocol validity → human-origin validity;
- two attestations → two independent causes;
- independent storage → independent generation;
- independent inspection → independent origination.

### L09 — Conjunction laundering

`A ∧ B → HUMAN_ORIGIN` is forbidden when A and B share the same causal source, merely restate the same proposition, or are jointly generated by the system under the counterfactual.

A conjunction is not a magic multiplier.
