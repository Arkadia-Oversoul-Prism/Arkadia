# CP-10.12 — HUMAN-ORIGIN VOCABULARY BOUNDARY

**Status:** SCHEMA-ONLY / VOCABULARY REFINEMENT / NO MECHANISM SELECTED / NO IMPLEMENTATION / NO ENFORCEMENT / REPOSITORY RUNTIME UNTOUCHED

**Parent chain:** `CP-10 → CP-10.1 → CP-10.2 → CP-10.3 → CP-10.4 → CP-10.5 → CP-10.6 → CP-10.7 → CP-10.8 → CP-10.9 → CP-10.10 → CP-10.11 → CP-10.12`

## 1. Purpose

CP-10.11 established a genuine protocol dependency:

```text
PRE-COMMITMENT → EVENT ADMISSION
```

It did not establish that the precondition was human-originated. The arena exposed two unresolved relations:

```text
PROTOCOL CAUSATION ≠ HUMAN CAUSAL ORIGINATION
INDEPENDENTLY INSPECTABLE ≠ INDEPENDENTLY HUMAN-ORIGINATED
```

CP-10.12 therefore does not propose another mechanism. It sharpens the vocabulary boundary around the unresolved question:

> **What would have to be observably different between a human-originated precondition and a system-generated precondition for a future mechanism candidate to discriminate the two without naming the mechanism in advance?**

The purpose is to define the epistemic burden without selecting the technology, mechanism class, trust anchor, sensor, credential, witness, device, cryptographic construction, biometric, enclave, or other implementation means that might eventually satisfy it.

## 2. Constitutional question

Can the architecture define the distinction between human-originated and system-generated preconditions precisely enough that a future mechanism candidate can be tested against it, without allowing the vocabulary itself to become a mechanism claim or an authority claim?

**Answer:** yes, if the vocabulary specifies the target relation, counterfactual burden, false-positive classes, and prohibited substitutions while leaving the positive observable mechanism unspecified.

## 4. Core vocabulary

### 4.1 Subject association

A subject is linked to an event, artifact, session, credential, channel, or state.

```text
ASSOCIATED(S, E)
```

Association does not imply interaction, intent, origination, authority, or execution.

### 4.2 Subject interaction

The subject participates in an interaction that is temporally or structurally related to the event.

```text
INTERACTED(S, A)
```

Interaction does not imply that the subject originated the resulting authority event.

### 4.3 Subject intent

A claim that the subject deliberately sought or intended a particular outcome.

Intent is a semantic proposition unless a future mechanism supplies an observable basis that discriminates the proposition from its false positives.

### 4.4 Subject origination

The subject is the originator of the specific event or precondition under evaluation.

```text
ORIGINATED(S, P)
```

This is the target relation. Naming it does not prove it.

### 4.5 Human causal origination

The subject's human act is the causal origin of the specific precondition, rather than merely an associated state, interaction, authorization, delegation, automation trigger, inherited capability, or downstream consequence.

```text
HUMAN_ORIGIN(S, P)
```

This is stronger than subject association and stronger than subject interaction. It remains unproven until a mechanism supplies an observable discriminator.

### 4.6 Authority

A normative or policy relation determining what the subject is permitted to cause or authorize.

```text
AUTHORIZED(S, X)
```

Authority does not establish origination.

```text
AUTHORIZED(S, X) ≠ ORIGINATED(S, X)
```

## 5. Human-originated precondition

For the purposes of this arc, a **human-originated precondition** is a precondition `P` such that the target relation is:

```text
HUMAN SUBJECT S
      ↓
causally originates
      ↓
PRECONDITION P
      ↓
which precedes / conditions
      ↓
EVENT E
```

The statement contains two distinct causal questions:

1. Did `P` actually condition `E`?
2. Did the human subject `S` causally originate `P`?

CP-10.11 supplied evidence toward the first relation. It did not independently establish the second.

Therefore:

```text
P → E
      ≠
S → P
```

and:

```text
(S → P) + (P → E)
```

must not be inferred merely because the protocol records both relations.

## 6. System-generated precondition

A **system-generated precondition** is a precondition that satisfies the protocol's formal requirements but whose causal origin is attributable to an automated, delegated, inherited, derived, or otherwise non-human-generating process rather than the subject's human act.

The vocabulary does not prescribe how such generation occurs.

Representative classes include:

- autonomous generation;
- authentication-triggered generation;
- delegated generation;
- replay or reproduction;
- inherited or pre-existing state;
- derived generation from another event;
- administrative or background process;
- synthetic generation that preserves all protocol-visible fields.

These are false-positive classes, not implementation requirements.

## 7. The decisive counterfactual

Every future mechanism candidate must be tested against two worlds.

### World H — Human-origin world

```text
S performs the relevant human act
        ↓
P is generated
        ↓
P satisfies protocol requirements
        ↓
E is admitted
```

### World S — System-origin world

```text
S is associated with the protocol context
        ↓
SYSTEM generates P without S performing the relevant human act
        ↓
P satisfies the same protocol requirements
        ↓
E is admitted
```

The decisive question is:

> **Is there an observable property available to the candidate that differs between World H and World S because of the human origination relation itself?**

If the candidate-visible evidence is identical in both worlds, the candidate has not discriminated human origination.

Formally:

```text
OBS(H) = OBS(S)
        ↓
NO HUMAN-ORIGIN DISCRIMINATION
```

Conversely, a future candidate must identify an observable difference that is causally relevant to the target relation rather than merely correlated with subject association, session ownership, authorization, interaction, or downstream execution.

## 8. Evidence states

The CP-10.7 evidence ladder remains authoritative:

```text
E1 — EVIDENCE PRESENCE
Evidence exists.

E2 — EVIDENCE DISCRIMINATION
Evidence differentiates the target from the relevant false positives.

E3 — EVIDENCE SUFFICIENCY
The required dimensions are satisfied without forbidden substitution,
contradiction, circularity, or downstream laundering.
```

For human-origin evaluation:

```text
E1 ≠ E2 ≠ E3
```

A valid precondition record may satisfy E1 while failing E2. A discriminating observable may satisfy E2 while still failing E3 if independence, counterfactual, or boundary requirements remain unresolved.

## 9. Human-origin evidence burden

A future candidate must not merely demonstrate that:

```text
P is fresh
P is single-use
P is subject-bound
P precedes E
P is scope-bound
P is inspectable
P is protocol-valid
```

Those properties establish protocol structure. They do not by themselves establish human origination.

The candidate must additionally provide an observable basis capable of discriminating:

```text
HUMAN-ORIGINATED P
        from
SYSTEM-GENERATED P
```

without assuming the proposition it is supposed to prove.

## 12. Mechanism independence boundary

CP-10.11 exposed the distinction:

```text
INDEPENDENTLY INSPECTABLE
        ≠
INDEPENDENTLY GENERATED
```

CP-10.12 therefore distinguishes:

```text
EVIDENCE CAN BE INSPECTED
        ≠
EVIDENCE WAS GENERATED INDEPENDENTLY OF THE SYSTEM
        ≠
EVIDENCE INDEPENDENTLY DISCRIMINATES HUMAN ORIGINATION
```

The vocabulary does not decide what mechanism would provide independence.

A future candidate must state its independence claim and expose it to arena testing. It may not receive independence merely because records are separately inspectable.

## 20. Final boundary

> **The vocabulary may define what human origination means, identify the false positives, and specify the counterfactual burden. It may not declare a protocol-valid, subject-bound, inspectable precondition to be human-originated without an independent observable discriminator. The mechanism must provide the observable. The arena must determine whether it discriminates. Human origination does not become authority. Authority does not become authorization. Authorization does not become execution.**

**CP-10.12 closes the ambiguity around the human-origin target. It does not close the mechanism frontier.**
