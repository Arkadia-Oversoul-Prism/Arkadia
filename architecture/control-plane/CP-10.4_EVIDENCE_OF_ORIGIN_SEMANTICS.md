# CP-10.4 — EVIDENCE OF ORIGIN SEMANTICS

**Status:** grounded / schema-only / normative / technology-neutral

## Core definition
Evidence of origin is evidence whose semantic referent includes the origination relationship between a defined subject and a defined authority event, within the applicable temporal and contextual boundaries, including deliberate-origin semantics where required.

## Origin evidence vector
`OE = [SUBJECT, EVENT, ORIGIN_RELATION, DELIBERATE_ORIGIN, TIME_CONTEXT]`

## Five dimensions
- D1 Subject referent: whose origin?
- D2 Event referent: origin of what exact event?
- D3 Origin relation: what connects subject to origination of the event?
- D4 Deliberate-origin semantics: was the event deliberately originated where required?
- D5 Temporal/contextual binding: did the relation occur in the applicable event context?

## Non-substitution
`identity ≠ origin`  
`authentication ≠ origin`  
`session ≠ origin`  
`interaction ≠ origin`  
`witness ≠ origin`  
`structural integrity ≠ origin`  
`execution ≠ origin`  
`approval assertion ≠ origin`  
`provenance label ≠ origin evidence`

## Negative state
`ORIGIN-UNPROVEN` is a legitimate state. A subject may be authenticated, an interaction may be real, an artifact may be intact, and execution may be possible or complete while authority-event origin remains unestablished.

No downstream state may upgrade ORIGIN-UNPROVEN to ORIGIN-PROVEN.

## Semantic test
Q1 whose origin?  
Q2 which exact event?  
Q3 what relation establishes origin?  
Q4 was deliberate origin established where required?  
Q5 what binds the relation to time/context?

If any applicable question cannot be answered, origin semantics are UNKNOWN.

## Grounding result
CP-10.4 closed the vocabulary gap. It did not close the provenance gap. Semantic evaluability passed; repository realization and collision resolution remained unresolved. The later arena proved that the positive D3/D4 relation requires an evidentiary mechanism rather than more vocabulary alone.
