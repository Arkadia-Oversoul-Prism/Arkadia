## 1. Candidate question

Does the unresolved human-origin problem imply a **mechanism-prescriptive boundary**: namely, that a positive instantiation of `HUMAN_ORIGIN(S,P)` cannot be achieved by mechanism-neutral vocabulary alone, because the discriminating observable must ultimately depend on some concrete property of how the relevant precondition is produced?

The candidate does **not** select such a mechanism. It tests only the architectural boundary between `MECHANISM-NEUTRAL SPECIFICATION` and `MECHANISM-PRESCRIPTIVE PROOF REQUIREMENT`.

The distinction under test is:

`A PROOF REQUIREMENT MAY BE MECHANISM-PRESCRIPTIVE ≠ A MECHANISM HAS BEEN SELECTED`.

---

## 2. Inherited problem statement

The provenance arc separates:

`CAUSAL CLAIM ≠ CAUSAL FACT ≠ EVIDENCE OF CAUSAL FACT ≠ PROVENANCE ≠ AUTHORIZATION ≠ EXECUTION`.

The target remains human causal origination, not mere association, authentication, attestation, temporal precedence, protocol dependency, or successful execution.

The decisive counterfactual remains:

### World H

A human act produces the relevant precondition `P`, after which the target event `E` is admitted.

### World S

A system, automation, delegated process, replay, inherited state, or other non-human process produces a protocol-valid `P`, after which the same target event `E` is admitted.

A candidate must identify an observable relation that differs between World H and World S for the relevant target claim.

---

## 3. Boundary hypothesis

If every protocol-visible property of `P` can be reproduced by World S while preserving the observations available to the verifier, then no mechanism-neutral semantic vocabulary can by itself discriminate `HUMAN_ORIGIN(S,P)`.

Therefore, positive discrimination requires at least one observable whose truth depends on a property of the production process that World S cannot reproduce under the defined threat model.

That requirement may constrain the class of admissible mechanisms without selecting one.

The proposed boundary is:

`MECHANISM-NEUTRAL CLAIM`

may define **what must be distinguished**, but cannot by itself guarantee **that the distinction is instantiated in the world**.

Conversely:

`MECHANISM-PRESCRIPTIVE REQUIREMENT`

may define that evidence must arise from some non-substitutable production property, while leaving concrete mechanism selection to a later human-governed stage.

---

## 4. Counterfactual analysis

Consider two worlds with identical subject, timestamp, session, event identity, protocol validity, authorization state, and execution result.

If the verifier receives no observable that depends on the difference between human production of `P` and system production of `P`, then the worlds are observationally equivalent with respect to the target claim.

No repetition of those same protocol-visible properties establishes human origination. This includes identity assertions, authenticated sessions, temporal precedence, interaction records, event ownership, delegation records, successful execution, and repeated corroboration of the same non-discriminating relation.

The boundary argument therefore claims only this:

> Positive causal-origin evidence requires at least one discriminating observable whose truth is not invariant under the relevant human/system substitution.

It does not claim that a particular technical mechanism is therefore required.

---

## 5. What the boundary argument establishes

The candidate establishes a **proof-shape constraint**, not positive proof of human origin.

Future positive candidates must answer:

1. What observable differs between World H and World S?
2. Why does that observable depend on production of `P` rather than a later protocol record?
3. Why can the system not reproduce it under the stated threat model?
4. What independence exists between the evidence and the mechanism being evaluated?
5. Which part is mechanism-neutral, and which part necessarily constrains implementation?
6. Where does human selection enter before implementation or authorization?

This separates:

`PROOF-SHAPE REQUIREMENT`
from
`MECHANISM SELECTION`.

---
